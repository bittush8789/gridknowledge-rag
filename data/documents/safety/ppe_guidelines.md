# Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) Guidelines for Grid Operations

--- Page 1 ---
## Section 1: Standard PPE Baseline for Substation Entry
Every individual entering any operational substation yard or switchroom must wear mandatory baseline PPE at all times:
1. Hard Hat: Non-conductive dielectric safety helmet conforming to ANSI Z89.1 Class E (tested up to 20,000 Volts).
2. Eye Protection: Safety glasses with non-metallic side shields conforming to ANSI Z87.1.
3. Footwear: Dielectric steel-toe safety boots rated to 18 kV EH (Electrical Hazard, ASTM F2413).
4. Apparel: Arc-rated flame-resistant (FR) long-sleeve shirt and trousers with a minimum rating of 8 cal/cm² (NFPA 70E Arc Flash Category 2). Synthetic materials such as polyester, nylon, and rayon are strictly prohibited under FR clothing.

## Section 2: Transformer Maintenance PPE Requirements
When conducting inspection, oil sampling, testing, or maintenance on power transformers:
1. Standard Transformer Inspection & Routine Servicing:
   - Category 2 Arc-Rated FR coveralls / workwear (minimum arc thermal performance value ATPV 8 cal/cm²).
   - Class E dielectric hard hat with attached arc-rated face shield (minimum 8 cal/cm²).
   - Dielectric EH safety work boots with oil-resistant soles.
   - Heavy-duty leather mechanical work gloves for handling radiator valves and inspection hatches.
2. Transformer Insulating Oil Sampling and Chemical Testing:
   - Chemical-resistant nitrile gloves (minimum thickness 8 mil) worn over or under mechanical work gloves to prevent direct skin contact with hydrocarbon transformer oil and PCB traces.
   - Chemical splash safety goggles conforming to ANSI Z87.1.
   - Neoprene chemical apron if draining or transferring bulk hot oil (> 50 liters).
3. Bushing Testing and High-Voltage Proximity Work:
   - ASTM D120 Class 4 dielectric rubber insulating gloves (rated for 36 kV phase-to-phase) equipped with top-grain leather protector gloves.
   - Category 4 Arc Flash Suit (minimum rating 40 cal/cm²) complete with arc hood, hearing protection earplugs, and double-layer thermal balaclava when working in de-energized transformer bays adjacent to live energized 33kV or 132kV overhead busbars.

## Section 3: Inspection and Certification of Insulating Gloves
Dielectric rubber gloves must undergo visual and pneumatic air leakage checks before each use. In addition, electrical dielectric re-testing must occur every 6 months in an accredited high-voltage laboratory. Gloves showing pinholes, cracking, ozone corona cuts, or chemical swelling must be cut into pieces and discarded immediately.
